Plated in Gold

What drives Apple smartwatch resale prices?

We analyze third-party Apple smartwatch listings, web-scraped from online marketplaces, to provide a snapshot of the market and learn which factors are most associated with list price. From the title we use regex to extract the model, premium markers like Hermès editions and materials like titanium and ceramic, and aftermarket additions such as 24k gold plating.

The largest caveat: these are list prices, not completed transactions — but they still show which product, seller and geographic characteristics are associated with higher or lower asking prices.

The question

Which product, seller and geographic factors are associated with higher or lower Apple Watch asking prices on resale marketplaces?

Modeling: CatBoost price model

Explaining the model with SHAP

SHAP comes from game theory: how to fairly distribute credit when many players contribute. For each listing it splits the gap between the model's average prediction and that listing's prediction into a positive or negative dollar amount per feature, averaging over the possible orderings in which features are added.

    Global feature ranking

    A CatBoost regressor plus SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) ranks features by how many dollars they move the predicted price, on average.

    Model is the top feature — but simple comparisons mislead.

    2
    A feature's effect varies across the market.

    3
    Country is a large source of variation.

    Limitations

    • Listings, not completed sales. The data has asking prices only; a high list price doesn't mean a watch sold for that amount.

    Future work

    • Analyze seller ID in more detail: do power sellers over- or under-price?
    • Re-scrape the same marketplaces over time to see how long listings stay up and to estimate sale prices.
